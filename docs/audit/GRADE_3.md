## 3-1 · Equal groups: meet × and ÷ (weeks 1–4, The Market)

*Multiplication as equal groups you can see and count — the idea the whole year stands on.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** Three friends walk up together. Each one wants 4 cups for their team.
> How many cups do you need to pour? — **12** → teaches *Multiplication facts*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** equalGroups, mulAsRepeatedAdd, divAsSharing

**Teach panels:**
- **Multiplication facts** — Multiplication is equal groups — skip-count the group, not the ones.
    - Say it as groups: 7 × 6 means seven groups of 6
    - Build from a fact you own: 7×6 = 7×5 + one more 7 = 35 + 7 = 42
    - Your turn: 8 × 6 = ? → **48**
- **Division facts** — Division asks "how many groups fit?" — it’s a times fact read backwards.
    - Turn it into a missing factor: 42 ÷ 6 asks: 6 × ? = 42
    - Answer with the fact: 6 × 7 = 42, so 42 ÷ 6 = 7
    - Your turn: 54 ÷ 9 = ? → **6**
- **Missing factor puzzles** — A missing factor is a times fact wearing a mask.
    - Read the box as a question: 4 × ▢ = 28 asks: 4 times what is 28?
    - Use the family: 28 ÷ 4 = 7, so ▢ = 7
    - Your turn: 4 × ▢ = 36 → **9**
- **Area as multiplication** — Area is rows times columns — multiplication you can tile and walk on.
    - Count rows and columns: A 4-tile by 6-tile mat: 4 rows of 6
    - Multiply: 4 × 6 = 24 tiles cover it
    - Your turn: A 3 × 9 floor — how many tiles? → **27**

**Days (16):** 4 new · 4 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 16 (quiz)

## 3-3 · Place value & big add/sub (weeks 9–12, Number Mine)

*Hundreds and thousands behave just like ones — add, subtract, and round them with confidence.*

**Snag (opens day 33):** Saturday the stand made 148 Kredits. Sunday it made 236. Mom asks what the whole weekend made.
> 148 + 236 = ? — **384** → teaches *Multi-digit addition*

**Teach panels:**
- **Multi-digit addition** — Line up the places, add right to left, and carry tens as they fill.
    - Stack by place value: 148 + 236: ones over ones, tens over tens
    - Add each column, carrying: 8+6=14 → write 4 carry 1; 4+3+1=8; 1+2=3 → 384
    - Your turn: 257 + 168 = ? → **425**
- **Multi-digit subtraction** — Line up the places; when the top digit is too small, borrow a ten.
    - Stack and check each column: 402 − 156: 2 − 6 can’t go — borrow
    - Borrow and subtract through: 12−6=6, 9−5=4, 3−1=2 → 246
    - Your turn: 523 − 247 = ? → **276**
- **Rounding** — Find the rounding place, peek ONE digit to its right: 5 or more rounds up.
    - Underline the place, peek right: Round 647 to tens: the 4, peek at 7
    - 5+ rounds up, else down: 7 rounds the 4 up → 650
    - Your turn: Round 3,482 to the nearest hundred → **3,500**
- **Two-step word problems** — Find the hidden question first — then the asked one is easy.
    - Spot what you must know first: 3 trays of 8 cups, 10 sold. Hidden: how many cups?
    - Do the steps in order: 3×8 = 24, then 24 − 10 = 14 left
    - Your turn: 4 boxes of 6 cups arrive; 9 sell. How many left? → **15**

**Days (16):** 4 new · 4 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 48 (quiz)

## 3-5 · Fractions as numbers (weeks 17–21, The Kitchen)

*A fraction is a number with a place on the line — not a pizza slice.*

**Snag (opens day 65):** Your last tray has 8 cookies. A customer says: "I’ll take half of them."
> What is half of 8? — **4** → teaches *Fraction of a set*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** fracNumberLine

**Teach panels:**
- **Fraction of a set** — A fraction OF a set: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.
    - Divide by the denominator: 3/4 of 12: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 (one quarter)
    - Multiply by the numerator: 3 × 3 = 9
    - Your turn: 2/3 of 18 = ? → **12**
- **Comparing unit fractions** — One piece each: MORE slices means SMALLER pieces.
    - Picture the same pizza cut both ways: 1/3 vs 1/5: thirds are bigger pieces
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 1/4 or 1/6? → **1/4**
- **Equivalent fractions** — Multiply top AND bottom by the same number — the amount keeps its size, only its name changes.
    - Scale both at once: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 — same lemonade, finer cuts
    - To rename, find the scale: 2/3 = ▢/12: bottom ×4, so top ×4 → 8
    - Your turn: 2/3 = ▢/12 → **8**
- **Comparing fractions** — Give both fractions the same bottom — then just compare tops.
    - Rename to a common denominator: 2/3 vs 3/5 → 10/15 vs 9/15
    - Compare tops: 10 > 9, so 2/3 is bigger
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 3/4 or 2/3? → **3/4**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 84 (quiz)
