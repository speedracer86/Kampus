## 7-1 · Proportional relationships (weeks 1–5, Trade Tycoon)

*When two things grow in lockstep — and how to use one to find the other.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** Karla leans on the counter: "That food blogger brings 3 new customers for every 2 posts. She’s posting 8 this week. Stock for it — or watch them walk."
> How many new customers should you stock for? — **12** → teaches *Solving proportions*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** fracUnitRate, propGraphs

**Teach panels:**
- **Solving proportions** — Two ratios in lockstep: find what one side was multiplied by — the other side gets it too.
    - Line up the proportion: 3 customers / 2 posts = x / 8 posts
    - Match the multiplier: 2→8 is ×4, so 3→12
    - Your turn: 2/5 = x/20. x = ? → **8**
- **Unit rates** — Divide to the for-ONE price — then every comparison is fair.
    - Divide price by count: $5.40 for 12 lemons → 45¢ each
    - Compare per-ones: $3.44 for 8 → 43¢ each: the 8-pack wins
    - Your turn: $3.60 for 8 lemons — price per lemon? → **45¢**
- **Scaling recipes** — Find the scale factor (new ÷ old), then multiply EVERY ingredient by it.
    - New ÷ old: 20 cups from an 8-cup recipe → ×2.5
    - Scale each ingredient: 2 cups sugar × 2.5 = 5 cups
    - Your turn: Recipe for 6 uses 3 eggs. Servings for 10 — how many eggs? → **5**

**Days (20):** 3 new · 3 guided · 9 mixed · 4 review · 1 quiz — ends day 20 (quiz)

## 7-2 · Rational number operations (weeks 6–10, Weather Station)

*Full command of negatives: gains, losses, and chains of both.*

**Snag (opens day 21):** Karla covers the ledger with her hand. "Week was +12, −5, +8, −9, −3. Before you peek: up or down, and by how much? An owner knows without looking."
> 12 − 5 + 8 − 9 − 3 = ? — **3** → teaches *Mixed integer operations*

**Teach panels:**
- **Subtracting negatives** — Subtracting a negative is ADDING — removing a debt makes you richer.
    - Two minuses make a plus: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3
    - Add: = 8
    - Your turn: 4 − (−6) = ? → **10**
- **Multiplying integers** — Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative. Then just multiply.
    - Settle the sign first: (−3) × 4: signs differ → negative
    - Multiply sizes: 3 × 4 = 12 → −12
    - Your turn: (−5) × 6 = ? → **−30**
- **Dividing integers** — Division keeps the same sign rules as multiplication.
    - Sign first, then divide: −24 ÷ 6: differ → −4
    - Your turn: −36 ÷ (−9) = ? → **4**
- **Mixed integer operations** — Walk the chain left to right, carrying a running total like a ledger.
    - One step at a time: 12 − 5 = 7, +8 = 15, −9 = 6, −3 = 3
    - Your turn: −5 + 12 − 4 = ? → **3**
- **Chained subtraction with negatives** — A chain of minuses: take them one at a time — no shortcuts, no panic.
    - First subtraction: 7 − 10 = −3
    - Keep walking: −3 − 4 = −7
    - Your turn: 3 − 8 − 2 = ? → **−7**
- **Adding with larger negatives** — Different signs: subtract the sizes, keep the sign of the bigger side.
    - Compare sizes: −14 + 9: 14 beats 9, so the answer is negative
    - Subtract sizes: 14 − 9 = 5 → −5
    - Your turn: −20 + 13 = ? → **−7**

**Days (20):** 6 new · 6 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 40 (quiz)

## 7-3 · Expressions & equations (weeks 11–15, Equation Lab)

*Rewriting before solving — the algebra habit that makes hard things easy.*

**Snag (opens day 41):** Four friends order the same thing: a $2.50 cup and a $1.75 cookie each. Your helper rings up all eight items one by one. Karla: "There’s a faster way, and it never miscounts."
> 4 × (2.50 + 1.75) = ? — **17.00** → teaches *Distributive property*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** inequalities

**Teach panels:**
- **Distributive property** — Multiply the outside number into EVERYTHING inside the parentheses.
    - Deal it to each term: 4(2.50 + 1.75) = 4×2.50 + 4×1.75
    - Add the pieces: 10.00 + 7.00 = 17.00
    - Your turn: Expand 3(x + 5) → **3x + 15**
- **Combining like terms** — Matching letters add their counts; plain numbers stick with plain numbers.
    - Group the matches: 3x + 2 + 5x: the x’s are 3 and 5
    - Add counts: 8x + 2
    - Your turn: Simplify 7y + 4 − 2y → **5y + 4**
- **Two-step equations with negatives** — Same two-step undo — the negatives just ride along.
    - Undo the constant: −2x + 6 = 14 → −2x = 8
    - Divide by the negative: x = 8 ÷ (−2) = −4
    - Your turn: −3x + 5 = 20 → **−5**
- **Two-step equations** — Peel the onion outside-in: undo the + or − first, then the ×.
    - Undo the add/subtract: 2t + 5 = 29 → 2t = 24
    - Undo the multiply: t = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
    - Your turn: 3x + 4 = 19 → **5**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 60 (quiz)

## 7-4 · Percent in the wild (weeks 16–20, Trade Tycoon)

*Tips, discounts, interest, markup — the percent math that runs a business.*

**Snag (opens day 61):** You run 20% off on $2.50 cups. At the register, 10% tax goes back on. A customer bets you a cookie it "comes back to exactly $2.50."
> What does the cup actually cost? — **2.20** → teaches *Discounts*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** markup, salesTax

**Teach panels:**
- **Tips and totals** — A tip is a percent of the bill — slide to 10% and build from there.
    - 10% is one slide: 15% of $12: 10% is $1.20
    - Add the pieces: + half of that (5%) = $0.60 → $1.80
    - Your turn: 20% tip on $35 = ? → **7.00**
- **Discounts** — Discount comes off first; tax goes on what you ACTUALLY pay.
    - Take the discount: 20% off $2.50 → $2.00
    - Tax the new price: +10% of $2.00 → $2.20 (not back to $2.50!)
    - Your turn: 10% off $40, then 5% tax — final price? → **37.80**
- **Simple interest** — Interest = principal × rate × time. Three numbers, one multiply.
    - Line up P, r, t: $200 at 5% for 2 years
    - Multiply through: 200 × 0.05 × 2 = $20
    - Your turn: $100 at 4% for 3 years — interest? → **12**
- **Percent change** — Percent change = the change ÷ the ORIGINAL, read as a percent.
    - Find the change: 40 → 50 changed by 10
    - Divide by where you started: 10 ÷ 40 = 25%
    - Your turn: From 20 to 26 — what percent increase? → **30%**
- **Finding the whole from a percent** — When you know the part and its percent, divide to rebuild the whole.
    - Part ÷ percent-as-decimal: 15 is 30% of what? 15 ÷ 0.30 = 50
    - Your turn: 12 is 25% of what number? → **48**

**Days (20):** 5 new · 5 guided · 6 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 80 (quiz)

## 7-5 · Statistics & sampling (weeks 21–24, The Stand)

*What a fair sample can and can’t tell you about the whole crowd.*

**Snag (opens day 81):** The new flavor got rated by five friends: 5, 5, 4, 5… and one 1 from Leo, who was in a mood. Karla: "Before you kill the flavor — what’s the real average?"
> Mean of 5, 5, 4, 5, 1? — **4** → teaches *Mean & median*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** sampling, compareDistributions

**Teach panels:**
- **Mean & median** — Mean shares everything evenly; median is the middle of the sorted list. Outliers fool the mean — never the median.
    - Mean: total ÷ count: 4,6,5,40,5 → 60 ÷ 5 = 12
    - Median: sort, take the middle: 4,5,5,6,40 → middle is 5. Which feels like a normal day?
    - Your turn: Median of 3, 9, 7 → **7**

**Days (16):** 1 new · 1 guided · 9 mixed · 4 review · 1 quiz — ends day 96 (quiz)

## 7-6 · Probability (weeks 25–27, The Stand)

*Putting a number on maybe — and betting the stand’s stock wisely.*

**Snag (opens day 97):** The forecast says 3 of the next 10 days bring rain. The fair is one of those days, and ice doesn’t keep. Karla: "Put a number on the risk, then decide."
> What is the chance of rain, as a percent? — **30** → teaches *Probability*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** compoundEvents

**Teach panels:**
- **Probability** — Probability = ways it can happen ÷ all the ways there are.
    - Count the ways: 3 rain days out of 10 days
    - Divide, read as a percent: 3/10 = 30%
    - Your turn: A bag holds 8 marbles, 2 are red. P(red) as a percent? → **25%**

**Days (12):** 1 new · 1 guided · 6 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 108 (quiz)
