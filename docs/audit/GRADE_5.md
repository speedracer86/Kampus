## 5-1 · Place value & powers of ten (weeks 1–4, Decimal Depths)

*Each place is ten times its neighbor — the dial that moves decimals.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** The bulk site sells 10 bags of lemons for $23.00. You need the price of ONE bag before you click buy — and the decimal point is daring you.
> $23.00 ÷ 10 = ? — **2.30** → teaches *Powers of ten*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** decimalPlaceValue

**Teach panels:**
- **Powers of ten** — ×10 slides every digit one place left; ÷10 slides right. Digits move — not the point.
    - Count the zeros, slide that many: 23.00 ÷ 10: digits slide right once → 2.30
    - Your turn: 4.7 × 100 = ? → **470**
- **Rounding** — Find the rounding place, peek ONE digit to its right: 5 or more rounds up.
    - Underline the place, peek right: Round 647 to tens: the 4, peek at 7
    - 5+ rounds up, else down: 7 rounds the 4 up → 650
    - Your turn: Round 3,482 to the nearest hundred → **3,500**
- **Comparing decimals** — Line up the decimal points and compare place by place from the left.
    - Pad to the same length: 0.6 vs 0.58 → 0.60 vs 0.58
    - First difference decides: 6 tenths beats 5 tenths → 0.6 is bigger
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 0.4 or 0.35? → **0.4**

## 5-2 · Decimal operations (weeks 5–9, Decimal Depths)

*Adding, multiplying and dividing money-numbers without fear.*

**Snag (opens day 17):** Cups cost $0.35 each and you want 40 of them. Your gut says "about a dollar forty?" Your gut is about to overspend the Bank.
> 40 × $0.35 = ? — **14.00** → teaches *Multiplying decimals*

**Teach panels:**
- **Adding decimals** — Stack on the decimal point and add like money.
    - Points in a column: 4.50 + 5.25: points aligned
    - Add normally: → 9.75
    - Your turn: 3.80 + 2.45 = ? → **6.25**
- **Multiplying decimals** — Multiply as whole numbers, then place the point by counting decimal digits.
    - Ignore the points, multiply: 0.35 × 40 → 35 × 40 = 1400
    - Count decimal digits and place: two decimal digits → 14.00
    - Your turn: 0.25 × 12 = ? → **3.00**
- **Dividing decimals** — Slide both decimal points until the divisor is whole — then it’s ordinary division.
    - Slide both the same amount: 7.2 ÷ 0.8 → 72 ÷ 8
    - Divide: 72 ÷ 8 = 9
    - Your turn: 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = ? → **9**

**Days (20):** 3 new · 3 guided · 9 mixed · 4 review · 1 quiz — ends day 36 (quiz)

## 5-3 · Adding unlike fractions (weeks 10–14, The Kitchen)

*Different denominators just need a common name first.*

**Snag (opens day 37):** One jug holds 1/2 gallon of lemonade, the other 1/3 gallon. The cooler holds exactly 1 gallon. Do both jugs fit?
> 1/2 + 1/3 = ? — **5/6** → teaches *Adding fractions, unlike denominators*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** fracEstimation

**Teach panels:**
- **Adding fractions, unlike denominators** — Different bottoms can’t add — rename both to a common bottom first.
    - Find a shared denominator: 1/2 + 1/3: sixths work for both
    - Rename and add tops: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6
    - Your turn: 1/4 + 1/3 = ? → **7/12**
- **Subtracting fractions** — Common bottom first — then subtract the tops.
    - Rename to match: 3/4 − 1/3 → 9/12 − 4/12
    - Subtract tops: 9 − 4 = 5 → 5/12
    - Your turn: 5/6 − 1/2 = ? → **1/3**
- **Comparing fractions** — Give both fractions the same bottom — then just compare tops.
    - Rename to a common denominator: 2/3 vs 3/5 → 10/15 vs 9/15
    - Compare tops: 10 > 9, so 2/3 is bigger
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 3/4 or 2/3? → **3/4**

**Days (20):** 3 new · 3 guided · 9 mixed · 4 review · 1 quiz — ends day 56 (quiz)

## 5-4 · Fraction × and ÷ (weeks 15–19, The Kitchen)

*Scaling recipes up and cutting them down — multiplication and division grow up.*

**Snag (opens day 57):** The recipe makes 8 cups and uses 2 cups of sugar. Market Day needs 20 cups, and sugar is the expensive part.
> How much sugar for 20 cups? — **5** → teaches *Scaling recipes*

**Teach panels:**
- **Multiplying fractions** — Multiply tops, multiply bottoms, then simplify.
    - Straight across: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12
    - Simplify: 6/12 = 1/2
    - Your turn: 1/2 × 4/5 = ? → **2/5**
- **Fraction of a set** — A fraction OF a set: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.
    - Divide by the denominator: 3/4 of 12: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 (one quarter)
    - Multiply by the numerator: 3 × 3 = 9
    - Your turn: 2/3 of 18 = ? → **12**
- **Dividing with unit fractions** — Dividing by a unit fraction asks "how many pieces fit?" — multiply by the bottom.
    - Count pieces per whole: 3 ÷ 1/4: each whole holds 4 quarters
    - Multiply: 3 × 4 = 12 quarters fit
    - Your turn: 2 ÷ 1/3 = ? → **6**
- **Scaling recipes** — Find the scale factor (new ÷ old), then multiply EVERY ingredient by it.
    - New ÷ old: 20 cups from an 8-cup recipe → ×2.5
    - Scale each ingredient: 2 cups sugar × 2.5 = 5 cups
    - Your turn: Recipe for 6 uses 3 eggs. Servings for 10 — how many eggs? → **5**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 76 (quiz)

## 5-5 · Volume & the coordinate plane (weeks 20–23, The Workshop)

*Filling boxes and naming points — space gets numbers.*

**Snag (opens day 77):** Two coolers, one choice: 30×20×20 cm, or 25×25×24 cm. Ice is money — which one holds more?
> Which cooler is bigger inside? — **The 25×25×24 one** → teaches *Volume*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** coordinateGraphing

**Teach panels:**
- **Volume** — Volume fills in layers: the base layer’s area, stacked height times.
    - Area of the base first: 30 × 20 cm base = 600 cm² per layer
    - Stack the layers: × 20 cm tall = 12,000 cm³
    - Your turn: A 5 × 4 × 3 box — volume? → **60**
- **Area as multiplication** — Area is rows times columns — multiplication you can tile and walk on.
    - Count rows and columns: A 4-tile by 6-tile mat: 4 rows of 6
    - Multiply: 4 × 6 = 24 tiles cover it
    - Your turn: A 3 × 9 floor — how many tiles? → **27**

## 5-6 · Expressions & order of operations (weeks 24–27, The Workshop)

*Why everyone must read a math sentence the same way.*

**Snag (opens day 93):** Your helper wrote the day’s take as 3 + 4 × 5 and announced "35!" The register says 23. One of them is lying.
> 3 + 4 × 5 = ? — **23** → teaches *Order of operations with parentheses*

**Teach panels:**
- **Order of operations with parentheses** — Parentheses first, then × and ÷, then + and −.
    - Inside the parentheses first: 2 × (8 − 3): do 8 − 3 = 5
    - Then the rest: 2 × 5 = 10
    - Your turn: 2 × (8 − 3) = ? → **10**
- **Order of operations** — × and ÷ go before + and −, reading left to right.
    - Multiply first: 3 + 4 × 5: the 4 × 5 happens first
    - Then add: 3 + 20 = 23 (not 35!)
    - Spot the mistake in a friend’s work: One of these lines is wrong — tap it.
    - Your turn: 20 − 2 × 6 = ? → **8**
- **Evaluating expressions** — Swap the letter for its number, then follow order of operations.
    - Substitute: x = 4: 3x + 2 → 3(4) + 2
    - Compute: 12 + 2 = 14
    - Your turn: 5x − 3 when x = 6 → **27**
- **Number patterns** — Find the jump between neighbors — the jump repeats.
    - Subtract neighbors to find the jump: 5, 8, 11: the jump is +3
    - Jump once more: 11 + 3 = 14
    - Your turn: 7, 12, 17, ? → **22**

**Days (16):** 4 new · 4 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 108 (quiz)
