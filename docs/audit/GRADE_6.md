## 6-1 · Ratios & rates (weeks 1–5, Trade Tycoon)

*Per-cup, per-hour, per-dollar — the comparison tool behind every good deal.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** Two lemon deals, one Bank: 12 lemons for $5.40, or 8 lemons for $3.44. They both look fine. One of them is wasting your money.
> Which deal is cheaper per lemon? — **The 8-pack (43¢ vs 45¢)** → teaches *Unit rates*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** tapeDiagrams

**Teach panels:**
- **Unit rates** — Divide to the for-ONE price — then every comparison is fair.
    - Divide price by count: $5.40 for 12 lemons → 45¢ each
    - Compare per-ones: $3.44 for 8 → 43¢ each: the 8-pack wins
    - Your turn: $3.60 for 8 lemons — price per lemon? → **45¢**
- **Simplifying ratios** — Divide both sides of a ratio by their biggest shared factor.
    - Find the common factor: 12:18 — both divide by 6
    - Divide both: 12:18 → 2:3
    - Your turn: Simplify 10:15 → **2:3**
- **Sharing in a ratio** — Add the ratio parts, divide the total by that, then deal the parts out.
    - Total the parts: Share $20 in 2:3 → 5 parts
    - Size one part, multiply: $20 ÷ 5 = $4 → shares are $8 and $12
    - Your turn: Share 24 cookies in the ratio 1:2 → **8 and 16**
- **Scaling recipes** — Find the scale factor (new ÷ old), then multiply EVERY ingredient by it.
    - New ÷ old: 20 cups from an 8-cup recipe → ×2.5
    - Scale each ingredient: 2 cups sugar × 2.5 = 5 cups
    - Your turn: Recipe for 6 uses 3 eggs. Servings for 10 — how many eggs? → **5**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 20 (quiz)

## 6-2 · Percent (weeks 6–9, Trade Tycoon)

*Out of a hundred: discounts, tips, and what the sale sign really says.*

**Snag (opens day 21):** The park cart hangs a sign: "25% OFF $2.00 CUPS." Your cups are $1.60. Kids at the stand are asking which is actually cheaper now.
> What does the park cart charge after the discount? — **1.50** → teaches *Percent of a number*

**Teach panels:**
- **Benchmark percents** — 10% is "divide by ten" — build every other percent from it.
    - Find 10% first: 20% of 45: 10% is 4.5
    - Scale up or down: double it → 9
    - Your turn: 10% of $38 = ? → **3.80**
- **Percent of a number** — "Percent of" means multiply: turn the percent into a decimal first.
    - Percent → decimal: 25% = 0.25
    - Multiply: 0.25 × $2.00 = $0.50 off
    - Your turn: 30% of 60 = ? → **18**
- **Finding the whole from a percent** — When you know the part and its percent, divide to rebuild the whole.
    - Part ÷ percent-as-decimal: 15 is 30% of what? 15 ÷ 0.30 = 50
    - Your turn: 12 is 25% of what number? → **48**
- **Fraction → percent** — Rename the fraction out of 100 — that count IS the percent.
    - Scale the bottom to 100: 3/4 = 75/100
    - Read the top: → 75%
    - Your turn: 2/5 as a percent → **40%**

**Days (16):** 4 new · 4 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 36 (quiz)

## 6-3 · Fraction ÷ fraction, decimal fluency (weeks 10–13, Fraction Factory)

*How many quarter-cups fit in three halves — division finishes growing up.*

**Snag (opens day 37):** Every cup needs 1/4 bag of ice. The freezer has 3 1/2 bags left, and the afternoon rush is coming.
> How many cups can you ice? — **14** → teaches *Dividing fractions*

**Teach panels:**
- **Dividing fractions** — Dividing by a fraction = multiplying by its flip.
    - Flip the divisor: 3 1/2 ÷ 1/4 → 7/2 × 4/1
    - Multiply across: 28/2 = 14
    - Your turn: 2 ÷ 2/3 = ? → **3**
- **Multiplying decimals** — Multiply as whole numbers, then place the point by counting decimal digits.
    - Ignore the points, multiply: 0.35 × 40 → 35 × 40 = 1400
    - Count decimal digits and place: two decimal digits → 14.00
    - Your turn: 0.25 × 12 = ? → **3.00**
- **Dividing decimals** — Slide both decimal points until the divisor is whole — then it’s ordinary division.
    - Slide both the same amount: 7.2 ÷ 0.8 → 72 ÷ 8
    - Divide: 72 ÷ 8 = 9
    - Your turn: 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = ? → **9**
- **Fraction → decimal** — Make the bottom 10 or 100 — or just divide top by bottom.
    - Rename over 100: 3/4 = 75/100
    - Read it as a decimal: 75/100 = 0.75
    - Your turn: 1/5 as a decimal → **0.2**
- **Decimal → fraction** — Say the decimal’s place value out loud — what you said IS the fraction.
    - Read it properly: 0.25 is "twenty-five hundredths" = 25/100
    - Simplify: 25/100 = 1/4
    - Your turn: 0.6 as a fraction → **3/5**

**Days (16):** 5 new · 5 guided · 4 mixed · 1 review · 1 quiz — ends day 52 (quiz)

## 6-4 · Rational numbers (weeks 14–18, Weather Station)

*Below zero is still a number — temperature, debt, and distance from zero.*

**Snag (opens day 53):** Monday the stand made +$8. Tuesday the rain spoiled stock: −$3. The ledger wants the week so far as ONE number.
> 8 + (−3) = ? — **5** → teaches *Adding integers*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** coordinatePlane

**Teach panels:**
- **Adding integers** — Adding a negative is a step DOWN the number line.
    - Start at the first number: 8 + (−3): start at 8
    - Step down by the negative: 3 steps down → 5
    - Your turn: −4 + 9 = ? → **5**
- **Comparing integers** — On the number line, RIGHT is greater — even below zero.
    - Place both on the line: −2 sits right of −7
    - Right wins: −2 > −7 (a $2 debt beats a $7 debt)
    - Your turn: Which is greater: −3 or −8? → **−3**
- **Absolute value** — Absolute value is distance from zero — distance is never negative.
    - Drop the sign, keep the size: |−6| = 6
    - Your turn: |−15| = ? → **15**
- **Subtracting below zero** — Subtracting more than you have drops you below zero — and that’s fine.
    - Walk the line past zero: 3 − 8: down 3 to zero, 5 more → −5
    - Your turn: 2 − 9 = ? → **−7**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 72 (quiz)

## 6-5 · Expressions & equations (weeks 19–24, Equation Lab)

*Letters stand for numbers you don’t know yet — and equations find them.*

**Snag (opens day 73):** Karla counts her day: "Two trays and 5 loose cookies — 29 cookies in all. So how many fit in one of my trays?" You realize you don’t know how to un-do her math.
> 2t + 5 = 29. What is t? — **12** → teaches *Two-step equations*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** exponentsIntro

**Teach panels:**
- **Evaluating expressions** — Swap the letter for its number, then follow order of operations.
    - Substitute: x = 4: 3x + 2 → 3(4) + 2
    - Compute: 12 + 2 = 14
    - Your turn: 5x − 3 when x = 6 → **27**
- **Evaluating two-variable expressions** — Two letters, same move: substitute both, then compute.
    - Swap both in: a=2, b=5: 3a + b → 3(2) + 5
    - Compute: 6 + 5 = 11
    - Your turn: 2a + 3b when a = 4, b = 1 → **11**
- **One-step equations (+/−)** — Undo adding by subtracting — from BOTH sides.
    - See what was done to x: x + 7 = 12: seven was added
    - Undo it on both sides: x = 12 − 7 = 5
    - Your turn: x + 9 = 14 → **5**
- **One-step equations (×/÷)** — Undo multiplying by dividing — both sides.
    - See the multiply: 4x = 28: x was times-four-ed
    - Divide both sides: x = 28 ÷ 4 = 7
    - Your turn: 6x = 42 → **7**
- **Two-step equations** — Peel the onion outside-in: undo the + or − first, then the ×.
    - Undo the add/subtract: 2t + 5 = 29 → 2t = 24
    - Undo the multiply: t = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
    - Your turn: 3x + 4 = 19 → **5**
- **Dependent & independent variables** — One variable drives, the other responds — write the rule as output = rule(input).
    - Name driver and responder: cups c drive money m: m = 2c at $2 a cup
    - Feed the driver: c = 6 → m = 12
    - Your turn: m = 2c when c = 7 → **14**

**Days (24):** 6 new · 6 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 96 (quiz)

## 6-6 · Area, surface area & volume (weeks 25–27, Equation Lab)

*Breaking shapes into pieces you already know how to measure.*

**Snag (opens day 97):** The fair booth is L-shaped: a 3×4 meter space with a 2×2 corner bitten out of it. The mat seller charges by the square meter.
> How many square meters is the booth? — **8** → teaches *Area as multiplication*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** triangleArea, nets

**Teach panels:**
- **Area as multiplication** — Area is rows times columns — multiplication you can tile and walk on.
    - Count rows and columns: A 4-tile by 6-tile mat: 4 rows of 6
    - Multiply: 4 × 6 = 24 tiles cover it
    - Your turn: A 3 × 9 floor — how many tiles? → **27**
- **Volume** — Volume fills in layers: the base layer’s area, stacked height times.
    - Area of the base first: 30 × 20 cm base = 600 cm² per layer
    - Stack the layers: × 20 cm tall = 12,000 cm³
    - Your turn: A 5 × 4 × 3 box — volume? → **60**

**Days (12):** 2 new · 2 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 108 (quiz)

## 6-7 · Statistics: the Stand unit + review (weeks 28–32, The Stand)

*Her own ledger becomes the dataset, then the grade test.*

**Snag (opens day 109):** Five day-profits: $4, $6, $5, $40, $5. Your helper announces "we average $12 a day!" You look at the list. Does $12 feel like a normal day?
> What is the MEDIAN day? — **5** → teaches *Mean & median*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** variability, histograms

**Teach panels:**
- **Mean & median** — Mean shares everything evenly; median is the middle of the sorted list. Outliers fool the mean — never the median.
    - Mean: total ÷ count: 4,6,5,40,5 → 60 ÷ 5 = 12
    - Median: sort, take the middle: 4,5,5,6,40 → middle is 5. Which feels like a normal day?
    - Your turn: Median of 3, 9, 7 → **7**
- **Percent of a number** — "Percent of" means multiply: turn the percent into a decimal first.
    - Percent → decimal: 25% = 0.25
    - Multiply: 0.25 × $2.00 = $0.50 off
    - Your turn: 30% of 60 = ? → **18**
- **Two-step equations** — Peel the onion outside-in: undo the + or − first, then the ×.
    - Undo the add/subtract: 2t + 5 = 29 → 2t = 24
    - Undo the multiply: t = 24 ÷ 2 = 12
    - Your turn: 3x + 4 = 19 → **5**

**Days (20):** 3 guided · 11 mixed · 5 review · 1 test — ends day 128 (test)
