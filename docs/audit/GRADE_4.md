## 4-1 · Place value to a million (weeks 1–4, Number Mine)

*Big numbers keep the same rules — comparing, rounding, adding and subtracting at scale.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** Fair weekend! The ledger says 1,286 Kredits on Saturday and 905 on Sunday.
> 1286 + 905 = ? — **2191** → teaches *Multi-digit addition*

**Teach panels:**
- **Multi-digit addition** — Line up the places, add right to left, and carry tens as they fill.
    - Stack by place value: 148 + 236: ones over ones, tens over tens
    - Add each column, carrying: 8+6=14 → write 4 carry 1; 4+3+1=8; 1+2=3 → 384
    - Your turn: 257 + 168 = ? → **425**
- **Multi-digit subtraction** — Line up the places; when the top digit is too small, borrow a ten.
    - Stack and check each column: 402 − 156: 2 − 6 can’t go — borrow
    - Borrow and subtract through: 12−6=6, 9−5=4, 3−1=2 → 246
    - Your turn: 523 − 247 = ? → **276**
- **Rounding** — Find the rounding place, peek ONE digit to its right: 5 or more rounds up.
    - Underline the place, peek right: Round 647 to tens: the 4, peek at 7
    - 5+ rounds up, else down: 7 rounds the 4 up → 650
    - Your turn: Round 3,482 to the nearest hundred → **3,500**
- **Multiplicative comparison** — "Times as many" multiplies the base — find the base first.
    - Name the base amount: Leo sold 7. "3 times as many" → base is 7
    - Multiply: 3 × 7 = 21
    - Your turn: Ava sold 4 times as many as Leo’s 6. How many? → **24**

**Days (16):** 4 new · 4 guided · 5 mixed · 2 review · 1 quiz — ends day 16 (quiz)

## 4-2 · Multi-digit × and ÷ (weeks 5–9, The Market)

*Batches times batches, and what the remainder really means.*

**Snag (opens day 17):** The school fair sends a real order: 24 boxes of cookies, 12 cookies in each box.
> 24 × 12 = ? — **288** → teaches *Multi-digit multiplication*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** factorsMultiples

**Teach panels:**
- **Multi-digit multiplication** — Break one number by place value, multiply each piece, add the pieces.
    - Split into tens and ones: 24 × 12 = 24×10 + 24×2
    - Multiply pieces and add: 240 + 48 = 288
    - Your turn: 31 × 14 = ? → **434**
- **Division with remainders** — Share into equal groups — the leftover is the remainder, and it means something.
    - Find the biggest fit: 50 ÷ 8: 8 × 6 = 48 fits, 8 × 7 = 56 is too big
    - Count the leftover: 50 − 48 = 2 → 6 remainder 2
    - Your turn: 38 ÷ 5 = ? → **7 r 3**
- **Multiplicative comparison** — "Times as many" multiplies the base — find the base first.
    - Name the base amount: Leo sold 7. "3 times as many" → base is 7
    - Multiply: 3 × 7 = 21
    - Your turn: Ava sold 4 times as many as Leo’s 6. How many? → **24**
- **Missing factor puzzles** — A missing factor is a times fact wearing a mask.
    - Read the box as a question: 4 × ▢ = 28 asks: 4 times what is 28?
    - Use the family: 28 ÷ 4 = 7, so ▢ = 7
    - Your turn: 4 × ▢ = 36 → **9**

**Days (20):** 4 new · 4 guided · 8 mixed · 3 review · 1 quiz — ends day 36 (quiz)

## 4-4 · Fraction equivalence & ordering (weeks 14–18, The Kitchen)

*Different names for the same amount, and how to tell which is more.*

**Snag (opens day 53):** Two pitchers on the counter: one is 2/4 full, one is 1/2 full. A kid insists the 2/4 pitcher has more.
> Is 2/4 more than 1/2? — **No — they are equal** → teaches *Equivalent fractions*

**Teach panels:**
- **Equivalent fractions** — Multiply top AND bottom by the same number — the amount keeps its size, only its name changes.
    - Scale both at once: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 — same lemonade, finer cuts
    - To rename, find the scale: 2/3 = ▢/12: bottom ×4, so top ×4 → 8
    - Your turn: 2/3 = ▢/12 → **8**
- **Comparing fractions** — Give both fractions the same bottom — then just compare tops.
    - Rename to a common denominator: 2/3 vs 3/5 → 10/15 vs 9/15
    - Compare tops: 10 > 9, so 2/3 is bigger
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 3/4 or 2/3? → **3/4**
- **Adding fractions, same denominator** — Same-size pieces: add the counts, keep the name.
    - Add numerators only: 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8 — eighths stay eighths
    - Your turn: 3/10 + 4/10 = ? → **7/10**
- **Mixed ↔ improper fractions** — A mixed number is wholes plus pieces — trade each whole for its pieces.
    - Trade wholes for pieces: 2 1/3: each whole is 3 thirds → 6 thirds + 1 = 7/3
    - Going back, pull out the wholes: 9/4 = 2 wholes (8/4) + 1/4 = 2 1/4
    - Your turn: 9/4 as a mixed number → **2 1/4**

## 4-5 · Fractions × whole numbers (weeks 19–22, The Kitchen)

*Three quarters, four times — fractions start doing work.*

**Snag (opens day 73):** By noon, 3/4 of your 12 muffins are gone.
> How many muffins sold? — **9** → teaches *Fraction of a set*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** fracTimesWhole, linePlots

**Teach panels:**
- **Fraction of a set** — A fraction OF a set: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.
    - Divide by the denominator: 3/4 of 12: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 (one quarter)

    - Trade wholes for pieces: 2 1/3: each whole is 3 thirds → 6 thirds + 1 = 7/3
    - Going back, pull out the wholes: 9/4 = 2 wholes (8/4) + 1/4 = 2 1/4
    - Your turn: 9/4 as a mixed number → **2 1/4**

## 4-6 · Decimals meet money (weeks 23–26, The Kitchen)

*Tenths and hundredths are coins — decimals arrive as money she already knows.*

**Snag (opens day 89):** The register wants $0.60. A kid holds out six dimes: "is that enough?"
> Six dimes, written as dollars — how much? — **0.60** → teaches *Decimal notation (tenths & hundredths)*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** addTenthsHundredths

**Teach panels:**
- **Decimal notation (tenths & hundredths)** — Tenths are dimes, hundredths are pennies — decimals are money you already know.
    - Read the places as coins: 6 dimes = 6 tenths = 0.60
    - Your turn: Write 3 pennies as dollars → **0.03**
- **Comparing decimals** — Line up the decimal points and compare place by place from the left.
    - Pad to the same length: 0.6 vs 0.58 → 0.60 vs 0.58
    - First difference decides: 6 tenths beats 5 tenths → 0.6 is bigger
    - Your turn: Which is bigger: 0.4 or 0.35? → **0.4**
- **Fraction → decimal** — Make the bottom 10 or 100 — or just divide top by bottom.
    - Rename over 100: 3/4 = 75/100
    - Read it as a decimal: 75/100 = 0.75
    - Your turn: 1/5 as a decimal → **0.2**
- **Decimal → fraction** — Say the decimal’s place value out loud — what you said IS the fraction.
    - Read it properly: 0.25 is "twenty-five hundredths" = 25/100
    - Simplify: 25/100 = 1/4
    - Your turn: 0.6 as a fraction → **3/5**
