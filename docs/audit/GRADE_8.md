## 8-1 · Exponents & scientific notation (weeks 1–4, Power Plant)

*Writing the enormous and the tiny without running out of page.*

**Snag (opens day 1):** Your stand video hit 1,200,000 views overnight. The ad network’s dashboard refuses numbers that long.
> Write 1,200,000 in scientific notation. — **1.2 × 10^6** → teaches *Scientific notation*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** exponentRules

**Teach panels:**
- **Exponents** — An exponent counts the multiplications — not a bigger times sign.
    - Write them out: 2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2
    - Multiply through: = 16
    - Your turn: 3³ = ? → **27**
- **Scientific notation** — One digit, a decimal point, times a power of ten.
    - Slide the point after the first digit: 1,200,000 → 1.2
    - Count the slides: six slides → 1.2 × 10⁶
    - Your turn: 45,000 in scientific notation → **4.5 × 10^4**

## 8-2 · Linear equations (weeks 5–9, Equation Lab)

*Solving anything linear — including equations with x on both sides.*

**Snag (opens day 17):** Karla slides a napkin across the counter: "New cart costs follow 3x + 12 = 45, where x is days to break even. Solve it before you sign anything."
> 3x + 12 = 45. What is x? — **11** → teaches *Multi-step equations*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** xBothSides, specialCases

**Teach panels:**
- **Multi-step equations** — Letters to one side, numbers to the other — one legal move at a time.
    - Collect the x’s: 5x − 7 = 2x + 20 → 3x − 7 = 20
    - Collect numbers, then divide: 3x = 27 → x = 9
    - Your turn: 4x + 3 = 2x + 11 → **4**
- **Two-step equations with negatives** — Same two-step undo — the negatives just ride along.
    - Undo the constant: −2x + 6 = 14 → −2x = 8
    - Divide by the negative: x = 8 ÷ (−2) = −4
    - Your turn: −3x + 5 = 20 → **−5**

**Days (20):** 2 new · 2 guided · 10 mixed · 5 review · 1 quiz — ends day 36 (quiz)

## 8-3 · Slope & linear relationships (weeks 10–14, Slope City)

*Steepness as a number: every line tells a rate story.*

**Snag (opens day 37):** Two Saturdays in the ledger: at $1.50 you sold 20 cups; at $1.00 you sold 30. Karla: "Every dollar you drop buys you how many customers? That number IS your business."
> Slope through (1.50, 20) and (1.00, 30)? — **−20** → teaches *Slope from two points*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** slopeIntercept, graphingLines

**Teach panels:**
- **Slope from two points** — Slope is rise over run between any two points — the per-one rate of the line.
    - Rise: subtract the y’s: (1.50, 20) to (1.00, 30): rise = 10
    - Run: subtract the x’s, divide: run = −0.50 → slope = 10 ÷ (−0.5) = −20
    - Your turn: Slope through (2, 3) and (4, 11) → **4**
- **Modeling with functions** — In y = mx + b, m is the per-one rate and b is where you start.
    - Read m and b as a story: y = 2x + 30: $30 to open, $2 more per cup
    - Your turn: In y = 5x + 12, what is the rate? → **5**

**Days (20):** 2 new · 2 guided · 10 mixed · 5 review · 1 quiz — ends day 56 (quiz)

## 8-4 · Systems of equations (weeks 15–18, Equation Lab)

*Two conditions at once — where the lines cross is the answer. Break-even lives here.*

**Snag (opens day 57):** The upgrade: $30 to open the new cart, $0.50 of cost per cup, $2.00 per cup at the register. Karla: "One question before the loan — how many cups until you’re even?"
> Solve: 30 + 0.5c = 2c. What is c? — **20** → teaches *Systems of equations*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** substitution, wordSystems

**Teach panels:**
- **Systems of equations** — Two equations share one truth — set them equal (or substitute) and solve once.
    - Set the two expressions equal: cost 30 + 0.5c meets income 2c
    - Solve the single equation: 30 = 1.5c → c = 20: the break-even
    - Your turn: y = x + 3 and y = 2x. x = ? → **3**

## 8-5 · Functions (weeks 19–23, Equation Lab)

*A machine that turns inputs into outputs — price in, demand out.*

**Snag (opens day 73):** Your pricing rule, learned the hard way, is d = 40 − 10p: cups demanded at price p. The fair fixes your price at $2.50 and wants your stock count tonight.
> d = 40 − 10(2.50). What is d? — **15** → teaches *Evaluating functions*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** compareFunctions, linearVsNonlinear

**Teach panels:**
- **Evaluating functions** — A function is a machine: feed it the input, follow its rule exactly.
    - Substitute the input: d = 40 − 10p at p = 2.50
    - Compute: 40 − 25 = 15 cups
    - Your turn: f(x) = 3x − 4. f(5) = ? → **11**
- **Modeling with functions** — In y = mx + b, m is the per-one rate and b is where you start.
    - Read m and b as a story: y = 2x + 30: $30 to open, $2 more per cup
    - Your turn: In y = 5x + 12, what is the rate? → **5**
- **Dependent & independent variables** — One variable drives, the other responds — write the rule as output = rule(input).
    - Name driver and responder: cups c drive money m: m = 2c at $2 a cup
    - Feed the driver: c = 6 → m = 12
    - Your turn: m = 2c when c = 7 → **14**

## 8-6 · Pythagoras & irrationals (weeks 24–26, Power Plant)

*The most famous theorem there is, and numbers that never resolve.*

**Snag (opens day 93):** The big sign needs a diagonal brace: the post stands 6 feet tall, and the brace anchors 8 feet from its base. The hardware store cuts to length — once.
> How long is the brace? — **10** → teaches *Pythagorean theorem*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** distanceFormula

**Teach panels:**
- **Pythagorean theorem** — In a right triangle: a² + b² = c². The legs squared build the diagonal squared.
    - Square the legs, add: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100
    - Root to finish: √100 = 10
    - Your turn: Legs 5 and 12 — the hypotenuse? → **13**
- **Approximating roots** — Trap the root between neighbor squares, then say which it’s nearer.
    - Find the neighbor squares: √50: 49 and 64 → between 7 and 8
    - Lean toward the closer one: 50 is barely past 49 → just above 7
    - Your turn: √30 is between which whole numbers? → **5 and 6**
- **Square roots** — The square root undoes squaring: what number times ITSELF makes this?
    - Ask the question: √81: what × itself = 81?
    - Answer from the squares you know: 9 × 9 = 81 → 9
    - Your turn: √64 = ? → **8**

## 8-7 · Bivariate data (weeks 27–29, The Stand)

*Two measurements at once — does hot weather really sell more cups?*

**Snag (opens day 105):** Ten days of temperatures and sales sit in the ledger. Karla: "Everyone SAYS heat sells cups. Your three hottest days sold 18, 22, and 26. Start with their average."
> Mean of 18, 22, 26? — **22** → teaches *Mean & median*

**Needs new generators (backlog):** scatterPlots, trendLines

**Teach panels:**
- **Mean & median** — Mean shares everything evenly; median is the middle of the sorted list. Outliers fool the mean — never the median.
    - Mean: total ÷ count: 4,6,5,40,5 → 60 ÷ 5 = 12
    - Median: sort, take the middle: 4,5,5,6,40 → middle is 5. Which feels like a normal day?
    - Your turn: Median of 3, 9, 7 → **7**
