08 · ADHD rules — binding, not vibes

Distilled from ADHD-friendly app design research (Monster Math's checklist + K-12 platform accessibility work), applied to this design. These override aesthetics when they conflict.

Untimed by default

Chill mode (progress bar, no clock) is the default everywhere. Blitz is opt-in, never shown first, and has no ticking sounds or last-seconds panic effects.

One primary action per screen

Every screen has exactly one dominant CTA (the green Play bubble). Everything else is quiet chrome she can ignore.

Focus dimming

When the teach panel opens, answers and chrome drop to ~45% opacity. The panel is the only warm surface on screen — attention has one place to land.

One step at a time

Explanations reveal one chunk per tap with the previous steps shrinking. Never a wall of steps. Every step readable aloud.

Progress for small wins

Question-progress bar in every round, tier stars, route path, weekly ticket meter — always something visibly filling.

Reward effort, not perfection

Tickets come from showing up (sessions, streak days, tier-ups) — never per correct answer, so there's nothing to grind and nothing to fail.

No punitive mechanics

Wrong answer = show the right one, no lost points, no lives. Failed grade test = a named practice plan + retry button, zero penalty.

Purposeful motion only

Animation fires as feedback (blob bounce, droplet burst on correct) and never ambient. Nothing loops, pulses, or moves while she's thinking.

CRA visuals in teaching

Number lines with jump arrows, fraction bars, dot groups — the representational layer appears before the symbols in every teach panel that supports it.

09 · Voice — Gen Z calibrated

The fastest way to lose a 13-year-old is trying too hard. The voice is understated, dry, and brief — it treats her like she's competent. Enthusiasm is shown through the blob and the animation, not through exclamation marks.

10 · Grade-adaptive design

Same components, different dials. The grade she's placed in switches a token set — a grade 3 kid and a grade 8 teen should feel like the app was made for them.

Grades 3–4 · "big & warm"
Dial settings Type +20% · buttons +25% taller · mascot large and on every screen · saturated floods · celebrations big (confetti, bounce) · copy ≤ 6 words, read-aloud offered on everything · Chill mode only by default · stats hidden from kid view
Grades 5–6 · "the baseline"
Dial settings The design exactly as shown in this guide. Balanced mascot presence, standard type scale, Chill default with Blitz visible, celebrations medium, tickets & streaks visible
Grades 7–8 · "tighter & cooler"
Dial settings Type −10%, denser layout · mascot shrinks to a corner sticker · muted floods, more ink · dark theme unlockable and default-offered · celebrations = one subtle glow, dry copy · her own stats visible (accuracy trend, best streaks) — teens love their own data
